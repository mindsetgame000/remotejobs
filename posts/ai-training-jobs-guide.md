AI companies need people to teach and test their models. That has created a fast-growing group of remote jobs called AI training, data annotation, rating or evaluation. The work is flexible and fully online, which makes it popular with students, writers and specialists who want extra income.

## Common types of AI training work

- **Data annotation and labelling:** tagging images, text or audio so a model can learn from it.
- **Response rating:** comparing two AI answers and choosing the better one, with a short reason.
- **AI writing and tutoring:** writing high-quality example answers in a subject you know well.
- **Red teaming:** trying to make a model give unsafe or wrong answers so the company can fix it.
- **Search and ads quality rating:** judging whether results match what a user searched for.

## Who gets hired?

Entry-level annotation is open to many people with good attention to detail. Better-paid projects look for **subject experts** (maths, coding, law, medicine), **strong writers**, and **native speakers** of languages other than English. A clear, careful writing sample is often the deciding factor.

## How much does it pay?

General annotation tasks often pay modest hourly or per-task rates, while expert projects for coding, maths or medicine can pay $30 to $60 an hour or more. Work volume can go up and down from week to week, so treat it as flexible income at first. ## Tips for passing the assessment

1. Read the guidelines twice. Most applicants fail because they skim the rules.
2. Take your time on qualification tasks; accuracy matters more than speed.
3. Write explanations in short, specific sentences.
4. Keep a record of the projects you join and the hours you work.

## Avoid fake AI job offers

Scammers copy the language of AI training jobs. Real platforms never ask you to buy software, pay a registration fee or "top up" an account to unlock tasks. Paste any suspicious message into our [job scam checker](/tools/job-scam-checker/), and apply through the company's own website.
