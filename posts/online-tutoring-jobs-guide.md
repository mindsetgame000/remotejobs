Online tutoring lets you teach from home on your own schedule, often in the evenings and at weekends. Demand is steady because families want extra help in maths, reading and test preparation, and adults keep learning languages online.

## Types of online teaching work

- **Subject tutors** help school students with maths, science, reading or writing, one-to-one or in small groups.
- **Test prep tutors** coach students for the SAT, ACT, GRE or IELTS.
- **ESL teachers** teach English to children or adults in other countries.
- **Instructional designers and curriculum writers** build the lessons and courses, usually as full-time remote jobs.

## What do you need?

Requirements vary a lot. Some tutoring companies only ask for strong subject knowledge and a degree, while school-partner programmes want a teaching licence. ESL roles often ask for a TEFL certificate. Almost every company checks your internet speed, your webcam and microphone, and a quiet place to teach.

## How pay works

Many tutoring jobs are **independent contractor** roles paid per hour taught, commonly between $15 and $30 an hour for general tutoring and more for specialised test prep. You are usually not paid for preparation time, so a $25-an-hour rate with 15 teaching hours a week is very different from a full-time salary. ## How to stand out

1. Record a short, friendly intro video if the platform allows it.
2. List the exact grades, subjects and exams you can teach.
3. Mention results: "helped 12 students raise their SAT maths score by 80+ points".
4. Keep your evenings and weekends open at first; that is when most students book.
5. Check time zones before you apply. Our [time zone converter](/tools/time-zone-converter/) shows when US students' evenings fall in your day.
