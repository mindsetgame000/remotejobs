A virtual assistant (VA) handles the everyday work that keeps a business running: email, calendars, travel bookings, research, simple bookkeeping and customer messages. Because almost all of it happens online, it is one of the most common ways people start working from home.

## What does a virtual assistant do?

Most VA roles fall into three groups:

- **General or admin VA:** inbox and calendar management, data entry, document formatting, booking meetings.
- **Executive assistant:** supporting one founder or manager closely, often with more responsibility and higher pay.
- **Specialist VA:** social media, ecommerce stores, real estate, legal or medical admin.

## Skills employers look for

1. Clear written English. Most of your work is written, so short, polite and correct messages matter more than anything else.
2. Comfort with common tools: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack, Zoom, Trello or Asana.
3. Reliability. Answering on time and doing what you said you would do is why clients keep a VA for years.
4. A decent typing speed. Check yours with our [typing speed test](/tools/typing-speed-test/); 40 words per minute or more is a good target.

## How much do virtual assistants earn?

Pay depends heavily on the country the employer hires from. Entry-level VA roles through staffing agencies often pay a few dollars an hour, while US-based executive assistant roles can pay $20 to $35 an hour. Use the [hourly to salary calculator](/tools/hourly-to-salary-calculator/) to compare an hourly rate with a monthly salary offer.

## A simple plan to get hired

1. **Pick a focus.** "General admin for small businesses" or "executive support for startup founders" is easier to sell than "I can do anything".
2. **Write a one-page resume** that lists the tools you know and one or two results, such as "managed a calendar of 40 meetings a week".
3. **Apply to real listings every day.** Browse the latest virtual assistant jobs, filter by the countries that accept you, and apply to five or more per day.
4. **Tailor every application.** Our free [cover letter generator](/tools/cover-letter-generator/) gives you a solid first draft in a minute.
5. **Prepare for a test task.** Many employers send a small paid or unpaid task, such as organising a messy spreadsheet. Treat it like real client work.

## Avoid VA scams

Real employers never ask you to pay for training, software or a "starter kit". If a recruiter moves the chat to Telegram or sends you a check to deposit, stop. Paste any doubtful message into our [job scam checker](/tools/job-scam-checker/) before you reply.
