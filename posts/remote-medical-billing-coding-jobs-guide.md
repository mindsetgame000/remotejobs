Medical billing and coding is one of the most popular ways to work remotely in healthcare. Coders turn doctors' notes into standard codes, and billers use those codes to send claims to insurance companies and follow up on payments. Both jobs are done on a computer, so many employers hire fully remote.

## Medical coding vs medical billing

- **Medical coders** read patient records and assign ICD-10, CPT and HCPCS codes. Accuracy is everything, because a wrong code means a denied claim.
- **Medical billers** create and submit claims, check why claims were denied, and contact insurers and patients about balances.

Many smaller practices combine both into one "medical billing and coding specialist" role.

## Which certifications do employers want?

Most remote coding jobs ask for a credential such as:

1. **CPC** (Certified Professional Coder) from AAPC, the most common for physician offices.
2. **CCS** or **CCA** from AHIMA, often preferred by hospitals.
3. Specialty credentials (for example risk adjustment or inpatient coding) for higher-paying roles.

Billing roles are more open to people without a certificate, especially if you have customer service or office experience.

## Typical pay

In the United States, entry-level coders often start around $18 to $23 an hour, and experienced or specialty coders can earn $25 to $35 an hour or more. Staffing agencies that hire for US clinics from the Philippines, Latin America or India pay less but offer a way in without US work authorisation. ## How to get your first remote coding job

1. Earn a recognised certificate and say so in your resume headline.
2. Apply for coding, billing, intake and "revenue cycle" roles, not just jobs titled "medical coder".
3. Highlight software you know, such as Epic, eClinicalWorks or Kareo.
4. Expect a coding test during the interview, and practise with sample charts beforehand.

## Watch out for training scams

Real employers do not sell you a course as a condition of hiring. Paying for a reputable certification from AAPC or AHIMA is normal; paying a "recruiter" for a training package is not. When in doubt, use our [job scam checker](/tools/job-scam-checker/).
